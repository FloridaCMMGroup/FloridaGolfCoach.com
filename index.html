How Florida golf is different

If you play most of your golf up north, Florida courses will surprise you. Here's what to expect.

Bermuda grass grabs the club
Florida fairways and rough are mostly Bermuda, which is wiry and sticky. Out of the rough, grip a little firmer and expect the ball to come out lower with less spin. Around the greens, a firm bump-and-run usually beats a delicate flop.
The greens have grain
Bermuda greens grow in a direction, and the ball follows it. Putts into the grain play slower and break less. Putts down-grain run out and break more. A shiny look usually means you're putting with the grain, a dull look means into it. Check the edge of the hole too: the ragged side shows which way the grain runs.
Wind is part of the game
Many Florida courses are open and flat, so the breeze moves the ball more than you're used to. Take more club, swing smoother, and keep the ball lower when it's blowing.
Lies can be firm and sandy
Thin grass over sand means the club can bounce. Ball-first contact matters more here than on soft northern turf.
